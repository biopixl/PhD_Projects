\subsection*{Clay reference standards}

We used the PBP01--PBP04 clay reference series for instrument calibration. Each series is a kaolinite-based clay matrix doped with certified Pb concentrations of 100, 500, 1000, and 0~ppm respectively (PBP04 is the blank). Three replicate preparations were made per series for each preparation method (pellet pressing and loose powder), yielding 12 pellet and 12 powder calibration points; one powder replicate (PBP04\_powder\_1) returned no detectable Pb peak and was excluded, leaving $n=11$ for powder calibrations. Replicate-level standard deviations within a series are well below the inter-series differences, supporting the use of all replicates as independent calibration points.

\subsection*{Stage A clay calibration: 4-arm performance}

For each preparation method, we fit a linear calibration of the form
$\widehat{\text{Pb}}_{\text{ppm}} = a + b \cdot \text{response}$
on the PBP standards. The fundamental-parameters (FP)-derived concentration was used as the response for the FP arms; the empirically selected line-intensity model (see Line Selection Sweep below) was used for the intensity arms. Pellet-intensity uses the multi-predictor Pb~L$\beta_1$+L$\beta_2$ model; powder-intensity uses Pb~L$\beta_1$+L$\beta_3$.

\label{tab:stage_a_calibration}
\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{llrrrr}
\toprule
Arm & Response & $n$ & Cal $R^2$ & Cal RMSE (ppm) & LOOCV RMSE (ppm) \\
\midrule
pellet$_{\mathrm{FP}}$         & FP-derived ppm                         & 12 & 0.995 & 27.6 & 32.7  \\
pellet$_{\mathrm{intensity}}$  & Pb~L$\beta_1$ + L$\beta_2$ (multi)      & 12 & 0.999 & 14.4 & 19.5  \\

The notable LOOCV inflation for the powder-intensity arm (97.9 $\to$ 361~ppm) reflects sensitivity of the multi-predictor fit to leaving any single PBP point out at $n = 11$; this is a calibration-sample-size limitation, not an instrument or response problem. \section*{S2. Ash-matrix correction and outlier handling}

\subsection*{Matrix-correction slopes (Stage B)}

For each calibration arm we fit a proportional regression
$\widehat{\text{Pb}}_{\text{ICP-MS}} = m_{\text{arm}} \cdot \widehat{\text{Pb}}_{\text{clay-pred}}$
on paired ash samples eligible for inclusion (i.e., excluding samples flagged by Cook's $D > 4/n$). The slope $m_{\text{arm}}$ absorbs the systematic clay-to-ash transfer bias arising from differing mass-attenuation coefficients of the two matrices.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\caption{Ash-matrix correction slopes and LOOCV stability. Eligible samples are those passing the Cook's $D > 4/n$ filter; excluded samples are still predicted (using the eligible-set full-fit slope) and contribute to the validation summary in main-text Table~3.}
\label{tab:matrix_correction}
\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{lrrrrr}
\toprule
Arm & $n$ eligible & $n$ excluded & Excluded samples & Full-fit slope & LOOCV slope SD \\
\midrule
pellet$_{\mathrm{FP}}$         & 29 & 3 & XPAH28, JPL73, XPAH20 & 1.130 & 0.056 \\
pellet$_{\mathrm{intensity}}$  & 31 & 2 & XPAH28, JPL73         & 2.711 & 0.092 \\
powder$_{\mathrm{FP}}$         & 33 & 3 & XPAH28, JPL73, XPAH20 & 0.334 & 0.023 \\
powder$_{\mathrm{intensity}}$  & 33 & 2 & XPAH28, JPL73         & 0.704 & 0.021 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

XPAH28 (ICP-MS Pb~$=$~18,528~ppm — an order of magnitude higher than the next-highest sample at 1,933~ppm) had Cook's $D$ values of 360--600 across the four arms, two orders of magnitude above any other sample. Its product of leverage ($h_i = x_i^2 / \sum x_j^2 \approx 1$) and squared residual was sufficient to dominate the proportional-regression denominator $\sum x^2$. JPL73 (804~ppm) was also flagged in every arm; XPAH20 (1,933~ppm) was flagged in the FP arms.

\subsection*{Outlier-handling sensitivity analysis}

We compared seven strategies for managing high-leverage samples in the matrix-correction step: no exclusion (all-sample LOOCV); concentration-based exclusion at fixed cutoffs (drop XPAH28 only, drop top-2, drop top-3, cap at 1000~ppm, cap at 500~ppm); and Cook's distance--based exclusion at the conventional $4/n$ threshold. All strategies validated on the complete paired set; only the slope-fitting subset varied. Cook's distance--filtered fitting yielded the lowest validation RMSE, smallest BA bias, and tightest LOA for the intensity arms, while preserving threshold sensitivity. Naive concentration-based dropping degraded performance.

\subsection*{Validation strategy comparison}

We considered three approaches to validating the matrix-correction step: leave-one-out cross-validation (LOOCV; the default), single 67/33 holdout repeated 100 times with random seeds, and 5-fold cross-validation also repeated 100 times. The intensity-arm RMSE estimates from the three strategies span similar central ranges, but the holdout estimates have standard deviations comparable to their means because XPAH28's leverage dominates whichever partition contains it. With $n \le 36$ paired samples, LOOCV is the most reliable estimator.

\small
\begin{tabular}{lrrr}

\midrule
