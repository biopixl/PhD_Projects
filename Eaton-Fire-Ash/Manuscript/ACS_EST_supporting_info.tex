\subsection*{Clay reference standards}

We prepared kaolinite-clay standards (PBP01--PBP04) with certified Pb concentrations of 0, 100, 500, and 1000~ppm. Three replicate pellets and three replicate powder samples were prepared per concentration level, yielding 12 pellet and 11 powder calibration points (one blank powder replicate was excluded due to no detectable Pb signal).

\subsection*{Clay calibration (Stage A)}

We evaluated two classes of XRF response variables for Pb quantification: (1) empirical L-line intensities and (2) the instrument's built-in fundamental-parameters (FP) algorithm. For L-line intensities, six Pb L-lines (L$\alpha_1$, L$\alpha_2$, L$\beta_1$, L$\beta_2$, L$\beta_3$, L$\beta_4$) were tested as single lines, summed-intensity combinations, and multi-predictor regressions. The L$\alpha$ lines overlap with As K$\alpha$ emission at 10.5~keV, which can bias Pb quantification in arsenic-bearing samples; L$\beta$ lines were therefore preferred.

Linear calibrations were fit on the PBP clay standards for each combination of sample preparation (pellet or powder) and response variable. Table~\ref{tab:clay_calibration} summarizes calibration performance for the four primary methods; calibration curves are shown in Figure~S1.

\subsection*{Validation on ash (Stage C)}

The clay calibrations were applied to ash samples and validated against ICP-MS Pb. Table~\ref{tab:response_comparison} ranks all candidate responses by validation RMSE. The pellet L$\beta_1$+L$\beta_2$ model achieved the lowest RMSE (151~ppm) with negligible bias ($-$2~ppm). The fundamental-parameters approach showed RMSE values 10$\times$ higher (1726--1880~ppm) with systematic positive bias (+308 to +319~ppm), likely because the instrument's built-in matrix corrections are optimized for homogeneous geological samples rather than heterogeneous ash.

\section*{S3. Matrix Correction}

Because XRF response differs between clay standards and ash samples due to matrix effects, we applied a correction factor derived from the paired XRF--ICP-MS data. Two samples with extreme Pb concentrations (XPAH28: 18,528~ppm; JPL73: 804~ppm) were excluded from correction factor estimation using Cook's distance criteria but were still included in validation.

\section*{S4. Sampling Protocol and Metadata}

\subsection*{Sampling campaign logistics}

The sampling campaign was coordinated between Caltech and NASA JPL field teams beginning January 16, 2025. Using a 1~$\times$~1~mile grid to guide spatially even coverage, teams collected ash samples within the burn scar and along the western, southern, and eastern flanks of the fire perimeter. A total of 169 samples were collected directly by field teams (95 by JPL, 74 by Caltech), supplemented by 52 samples from trained community volunteers using standardized sampling kits. Within each grid cell, sites were selected based on: (1) public accessibility, (2) visible ash accumulation, and (3) absence of debris removal activities. As sampling began approximately two weeks post-ignition, ash had accumulated in wind-traps on impervious surfaces (sidewalks against fences, street gutters). Both coarse ash fragments and fine soot were collected to obtain representative composite samples.

Major elements (Ca, Fe, Al, K, Mg, Na, Ti, Mn) showed relatively low coefficients of variation (CV = 31--126\%), consistent with geogenic contributions from the granitoid bedrock of the San Gabriel Mountains. The uniformity of these lithogenic elements across sampling locations rules out pre-fire soil heterogeneity as the source of metal variability observed in hazardous trace metals.
