\section*{S1. XRF Calibration Pipeline}

The XRF calibration followed a three-stage workflow: \textbf{Stage A} (clay calibration) established the relationship between Pb concentration and XRF response using reference standards; \textbf{Stage B} (matrix correction) derived multiplicative correction factors from paired XRF--ICP-MS ash data to account for matrix differences between clay and ash; \textbf{Stage C} (validation) applied the corrected models to ash samples and evaluated agreement with ICP-MS reference values. This section describes Stages A and B; Stage C results are presented in \S2.

\subsubsection*{Response variable selection}

We evaluated two classes of XRF response variables for Pb quantification: (1) empirical L-line intensities and (2) the instrument's built-in fundamental-parameters (FP) algorithm. For L-line intensities, six Pb L-lines (L$\alpha_1$, L$\alpha_2$, L$\beta_1$, L$\beta_2$, L$\beta_3$, L$\beta_4$) were tested as single lines, summed-intensity combinations, and multi-predictor regressions. The L$\alpha$ lines overlap with As K$\alpha$ emission at 10.5~keV, which can bias Pb quantification in arsenic-bearing samples; L$\beta$ lines were therefore preferred.

Table~\ref{tab:clay_calibration} presents clay calibration performance for all candidate response variables. The pellet L$\beta_1$+L$\beta_2$ multi-predictor model achieved the best calibration fit ($R^2$ = 0.999, RMSE = 14~ppm). Calibration curves for the four primary methods are shown in Figure~S1.

\subsubsection*{Calibration model equations}

The calibration models relate known Pb concentration (ppm) to XRF response. For L-line intensity models, the multi-predictor formula is:
\begin{equation}
\text{Pb}_\text{clay} = \beta_0 + \beta_1 \cdot \text{L}\beta_1\text{(cps)} + \beta_2 \cdot \text{L}\beta_x\text{(cps)}
\label{eq:intensity_model}
\end{equation}
where L$\beta_x$ is L$\beta_2$ for pellet or L$\beta_3$ for powder. The fitted coefficients are:

\textbf{Pellet} (L$\beta_1$+L$\beta_2$):
\begin{equation}
\text{Pb}_\text{clay} = -16.4 - 70.7 \cdot \text{L}\beta_1 + 151.5 \cdot \text{L}\beta_2
\label{eq:pellet_model}
\end{equation}

\textbf{Powder} (L$\beta_1$+L$\beta_3$):
\begin{equation}
\text{Pb}_\text{clay} = -52.6 + 430.2 \cdot \text{L}\beta_1 - 3327.1 \cdot \text{L}\beta_3
\label{eq:powder_model}
\end{equation}

The opposing signs of the L-line coefficients reflect collinearity between the predictors; the net effect produces accurate Pb estimates despite the counter-intuitive individual slopes.

For fundamental parameters (FP), the instrument's internal algorithm provides direct ppm values that are corrected via simple linear regression:
\begin{equation}
\text{Pb}_\text{clay} = \beta_0 + \beta_1 \cdot \text{FP}_\text{raw}\text{(ppm)}
\label{eq:fp_model}
\end{equation}

\textbf{Pellet} (FP):
\begin{equation}
\text{Pb}_\text{clay} = -27.2 + 0.326 \cdot \text{FP}_\text{raw}
\label{eq:pellet_fp_model}
\end{equation}

\textbf{Powder} (FP):
\begin{equation}
\text{Pb}_\text{clay} = -41.1 + 0.785 \cdot \text{FP}_\text{raw}
\label{eq:powder_fp_model}
\end{equation}

The FP slopes $<1$ indicate the instrument over-reports Pb in the clay matrix; calibration corrects this systematic bias.

\subsection*{Stage B: Matrix correction}

Because XRF response differs between clay standards and ash samples due to matrix effects, we derived proportional correction factors from paired XRF--ICP-MS ash data. The clay-calibrated models were applied to ash samples, and the slope of XRF-predicted vs.\ ICP-MS Pb concentration provided the matrix correction factor for each method. High-leverage samples were identified using Cook's distance ($D > 4/n$) and excluded from correction factor estimation but retained in subsequent validation. The number of excluded samples was method-specific: for L$\beta$ intensity methods, two samples were excluded (XPAH28: 18,528~ppm; JPL73: 804~ppm); for fundamental-parameters methods, three samples were excluded (XPAH28, XPAH20: 1,933~ppm, and JPL73).

The clay calibration models have slightly negative y-intercepts, which can extrapolate to negative Pb predictions for ash samples with very low true Pb concentrations ($<$30~ppm). These negative values are retained rather than clipped to zero because: (1) they affect only 1--2 samples per method (e.g., XPAH58 at 14~ppm ICP-MS Pb, which has anomalously low L-line intensities due to its calcium-rich matrix), (2) samples with small $|x|$ contribute minimal leverage in proportional regression, and (3) removing floor-clipping has negligible impact on validation metrics ($<$0.5\% change in RMSE).

\subsection*{Threshold classification}

Classification performance was evaluated at six regulatory screening levels: 80~ppm (DTSC residential), 200~ppm (EPA residential RSL), 320~ppm (California CHHSL industrial), 500~ppm (DTSC industrial), 800~ppm (EPA industrial RSL), and 1000~ppm (California TTLC hazardous waste).

For each threshold, samples were classified as positive (above threshold) or negative (below threshold) based on both ICP-MS reference values and XRF predictions. Performance metrics are defined as:
\begin{align}
\text{Sensitivity} &= \frac{\text{TP}}{\text{TP} + \text{FN}} \quad \text{(proportion of true positives correctly identified)} \label{eq:sens} \\[0.5em]
\text{Specificity} &= \frac{\text{TN}}{\text{TN} + \text{FP}} \quad \text{(proportion of true negatives correctly identified)} \label{eq:spec} \\[0.5em]
\text{Accuracy} &= \frac{\text{TP} + \text{TN}}{\text{TP} + \text{TN} + \text{FP} + \text{FN}} \quad \text{(overall correct classification rate)} \label{eq:acc}
\end{align}
where TP = true positive (ICP-MS and XRF both above threshold), TN = true negative (both below), FP = false positive (XRF above, ICP-MS below), and FN = false negative (XRF below, ICP-MS above). For screening applications, high sensitivity is prioritized to minimize false negatives (missed contamination).

\section*{S3. Sampling Protocol and Metadata}

\subsection*{Sampling campaign logistics}

The sampling campaign was coordinated between Caltech and NASA JPL field teams beginning January 16, 2025. Using a 1~$\times$~1~mile grid to guide spatially even coverage, teams collected ash samples within the burn scar and along the western, southern, and eastern flanks of the fire perimeter. A total of 169 samples were collected directly by field teams (95 by JPL, 74 by Caltech), supplemented by 52 samples from trained community volunteers using standardized sampling kits. Within each grid cell, sites were selected based on: (1) public accessibility, (2) visible ash accumulation, and (3) absence of debris removal activities. As sampling began approximately two weeks post-ignition, ash had accumulated in wind-traps on impervious surfaces (sidewalks against fences, street gutters). Both coarse ash fragments and fine soot were collected to obtain representative composite samples.
