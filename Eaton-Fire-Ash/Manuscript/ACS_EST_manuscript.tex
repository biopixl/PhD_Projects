\title{Rapid detection of metal contamination in ash on outdoor surfaces after the 2025 Eaton Fire in a Wildland-Urban Interface}

% ============================================================================
% ABSTRACT (150-200 words)
% ============================================================================

\begin{abstract}
Wildland–Urban Interface (WUI) fires combust a heterogeneous mix of vegetation and urban materials, generating ash that can contain elevated concentrations of toxic metals, posing potential post-fire exposure risks. Despite increasing frequency and severity of WUI fires, metal contamination in coarse outdoor ash remains poorly characterized, and rapid screening tools suitable for appropriate emergency response are limited. Here, we quantified the concentration and spatial variability of metals in ash collected from outdoor impervious surfaces following the 2025 Eaton Fire in Los Angeles County, California, and evaluated X‑ray fluorescence (XRF) spectroscopy for rapid field screening of lead (Pb). Thirty‑nine ash samples were analyzed by inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry (ICP‑MS), and paired subsamples were used to develop site‑specific XRF calibration models. Lead concentrations ranged from 14 to 18,528 ppm and exhibited extreme spatial heterogeneity (coefficient of variation = 464\%), with the highest concentrations from samples collected within the urban burn perimeter. Empirical calibration using cumulative Pb L‑line intensities showed strong in-sample correlation with ICP‑MS ($R^2$ = 0.994); however, leave-one-out cross-validation revealed reduced generalization to held-out samples ($R^2_{cv}$ = 0.84; $RMSE_{cv}$ = 1,232 ppm), with model stability sensitive to high-concentration samples. Bland-Altman analysis showed 95\% limits of agreement from $-$704 to +532 ppm. Despite these quantitative limitations, binary threshold classification was robust: when evaluated against regulatory screening thresholds, the calibrated model achieved 100\% sensitivity and specificity at $\geq$400 ppm, though sensitivity declined to 86\% at 200 ppm and 70\% at 80 ppm. These results demonstrate that site‑calibrated portable XRF reliably \textit{classifies} ash relative to high-level regulatory thresholds ($\geq$400 ppm) but should not be used for quantitative Pb determination or screening at lower thresholds without ICP-MS confirmation. We recommend reporting cross-validated metrics and threshold-specific classification performance when validating field screening tools for regulatory applications.\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

% Introduction should establish context, identify knowledge gaps, and state objectives
% Typically 3-5 paragraphs, ~500-800 words

The Wildland-Urban Interface (WUI) is the transition zone between wildland vegetation and the urban build environment, characterized by a complex mosaic of land cover and land use.\cite{radeloff2018wui} Fires in the WUI are fueled by a heterogeneous pre‑fire mosaic that includes vegetation (e.g., evergreen forest and chaparral) as well as manufactured materials from housing, vehicles, and urban infrastructure. In contrast to non‑WUI wildfires, which are fueled predominantly by vegetation, WUI fires involve the partial or complete combustion of anthropogenic materials such as structures and vehicles, resulting in the release of toxic compounds, including heavy metals.\cite{young2004hazards,jech2024marshall,villarruel2024bioaccessible,pereira2010heavymetals,fernandezmarcos2022toxics,terzano2021fireeffects,abraham2017postfire,alshehri2023nanomaterials,alexakis2020suburban} Despite their increasing frequency and severity, the environmental impacts of WUI wildfires remain poorly constrained, as highlighted by a recent U.S. National Academies report.\cite{nasem2022wuichemistry} Metals present in pre‑fire WUI landscapes can volatilize at high combustion temperatures and subsequently condense onto ash particle surfaces during cooling.\cite{senior2000vaporization} The contamination potential of wildfire ash is influenced by burn severity, fuel type, ash load, post‑fire climatic conditions, surface hydrology, and ash physical properties.\cite{sanchezgarcia2023ashchemistry} Trace metal concentration in ash is highly dependent on the metal content and composition of the combusted materials.\cite{pereira2010heavymetals,abraham2017postfire,etiegni1991woodash,campos2016eucalyptpine} For example, legacy lead‑based paint in pre‑1978 structures increases both the magnitude and spatial extent of lead emissions during WUI fires.\cite{schissel2025emissions} Few studies have focused on metal concentrations in outdoor ash from WUI fires which often burn older structures.

Lead (Pb) is a toxic metal that can cause damage to almost all organs and organ systems, with adverse health effects documented even at very low exposure levels.\cite{tong2000leadexposure,jusko2008bloodlead,levin2008leadexposures} The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) Regional Screening Levels (RSLs) for residential soil set a Pb threshold of 200 ppm, while California's Total Threshold Limit Concentration (TTLC) for hazardous waste classification is 1000 ppm.\cite{epa2024residential,dtsc2024ttlc} Despite increasing WUI fire frequency, no standardized field assessment protocol exists for ash contamination on outdoor public surfaces, and laboratory analysis via ICP-MS requires days to weeks---a delay during which ash remains mobile and accessible to returning residents.

The 2025 Eaton Fire, a WUI fire in Los Angeles County, was the second most destructive fire in California's history destroying over 9,000 structures in the foothill communities of the San Gabriel Mountains including Altadena, Kinneloa Mesa, and Sierra Madre. The fire ignited on January 7, 2025, and expanded to over 10,600 within 24 hours, with most of the catastrophic damage occurring gin the first 36 hours \cite{calfire2025eaton}. An unusually extreme Santa Ana wind event forecasted by the National Weather Society realized sustained winds above 90 MPH and wind gusts over 110 MPH descending from the canyons.\cite{nws2025wind} No progress on containment was reported until January 12 as the fire perimeter expanded northward into the San Gabriel Mountains. The Eaton Fire lasted 24 days (January 7-31, 2025) and burned an area larger than 14,000 acres leading to an estimated \$27.5 billion USD in damages.

The smoke plume from the Eaton Fire travelled South-West and deposited coarse, residual ashes for miles over the western coastline. Similar WUI fires have been studied to understand the post-fire environmental contamination of ash and soil with toxic concentrations of metals.\cite{jech2024marshall,villarruel2024bioaccessible} During the 2025 Los Angeles Fires, air quality monitors positioned miles away from the Eaton fire recorded a 110-fold increase in the hourly lead (Pb) concentrations of particulate matter particles with a diameter less than 2.5 µm (PM2.5) compared to pre-fire days.\cite{baliaka2025mmwr} This collaborative effort emerged to respond to a gap in our knowledge about the composition and distribution of metals in the coarse ash fraction remaining on outdoor surfaces of the post-fire landscape. 

We conducted extensive, systematic sampling of outdoor ash accumulated on impervious surfaces (e.g., sidewalk, curbs) both within the burn perimeter and downwind areas where no structures were destroyed. We hypothesized that the ash samples collected within the burn perimeter would contain higher concentrations of Pb and other metals due to proximity to combusted urban infrastructure. The friable nature of ash samples in the field necessitated rapid sampling to avoid areas impacted by cleanup activities and prior to rainfall. This study of the 2025 Eaton Fire provides a framework for assessing potential outdoor environmental exposure to ash from WUI fires. This study addresses three objectives: (1) quantify the concentration and spatial variability of metals in outdoor ash following the 2025 Eaton Fire; (2) develop and evaluate site-specific X-ray fluorescence (XRF) calibration models for Pb; and (3) assess the utility of calibrated XRF measurements for rapid screening against regulatory Pb thresholds.

\section{Materials and Methods}

The sampling campaign was initiated on January 16, 2025, with most samples collected prior to the first post-fire rainfall. Sampling was conducted by teams from Caltech and JPL, which initially sampled independently but coordinated efforts early in the campaign. A 1 × 1 mile grid overlay was developed to guide spatial sampling, with each grid cell assigned a unique numeric identifier. Using this grid to guide a spatially even sampling approach, teams traveled between grid cells collecting ash samples within the burn scar as well as along the western, southern, and eastern flanks of the Eaton Fire perimeter. Within each grid cell, the specific sample sites were selected based on three criteria: (1) public accessibility, (2) presence of visible ash accumulation, and (3) minimal post-fire disturbance from cleanup activities. 

A total of 169 physical samples were collected directly by field teams (95 by JPL and 74 by Caltech), supplemented by an additional 52 samples contributed from Caltech community volunteers. Because sampling began approximately two weeks after ignition, ash had largely accumulated in small wind-traps on impervious surfaces (e.g., on sidewalks against a fence or along street gutters). At each site, areas of concentrated ash deposition were identified, and approximately 5-15 mL of ash was collected. Ash was collected using small, non-metallic tools to minimize contamination (Figure 2A). For each sample, both coarse ash fragments and fine soot or dust were collected to obtain a representative composite sample (Figure 2B). Although efforts were made to collect only post-fire ash material, some mixing with background dust was unavoidable.

Thirty-nine ash samples (collected 16--25 January 2025) were selected for their spatial distribution and temporal representativeness (pre-rainfall) to proceed with elemental analysis by inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry (ICP-MS). Additional opportunistic samples and preparation details are provided in the Supporting Information. We followed EPA Method 6200 and 6020B protocols in selecting sieve sizes appropriate for the determination of elemental concentrations in soil and sediment.\cite{epa6200,epa6020b} Bulk ash samples were pulverized and homogenized to grain-size $<$250~µm (Figure~2C).

Thirty-seven paired subsamples of the 39 measured by ICP-MS were aliquoted for parallel Pb content analysis with a benchtop energy-dispersive X-ray fluorescence (XRF) spectrometer (INAM Expert 3L, RPE Institute of Analytical Control Methods, LLC). The paired XRF sample was prepared in two ways where material allowed: as a homogenized loose powder mounted in a polymer film cup ($n = 54$) and as a pressed pellet ($n = 53$). To minimize grain-size effects on XRF spectra, pellet subsamples were homogenized by grinding and sieving to $<$106~µm, mixed with cellulose binder (Corporation), and pressed into 13-mm diameter pellets (Figure~2D). XRF spectra from each sample preparation were acquired over 60~seconds of exposure with automatic peak deconvolution and matrix correction via the instrument's fundamental parameters (FP) software, which reported semi-quantitative concentrations in mass fraction percent (m.f.\%). For each measurement we additionally retained the per-line emission intensities (counts~s$^{-1}$) for the six Pb~L-lines reported by the instrument (L$\alpha_{1}$, L$\alpha_{2}$, L$\beta_{1}$, L$\beta_{2}$, L$\beta_{3}$, L$\beta_{4}$), excluding lines that overlap matrix elements (e.g., the As~K$\alpha$/Pb~L$\alpha_{1}$ overlap at $\sim$10.55~keV). An empirical sweep over single-line, summed-line, and multi-predictor combinations of these six lines was used to identify the response with the lowest matrix-corrected validation RMSE for each preparation (full sweep in Supporting Information): the L$\beta_{1}$+L$\beta_{2}$ multi-predictor was best for pellets and the L$\beta_{1}$+L$\beta_{3}$ multi-predictor was best for powders. This yields four candidate models to predict Pb in ash: pellet best-line intensity, pellet FP concentration, powder best-line intensity, and powder FP concentration. Given known limitations of fundamental-parameters models for heterogeneous ash matrices,\cite{ravansari2020pxrf} external calibration standards were prepared with known concentrations of Pb in kaolinite clay, for which a detailed protocol is included in the Supporting Information.

Each of the four candidate models was validated against ICP-MS Pb on 37 paired ash subsamples (33 with all three measurements; 34 pellet, 36 powder). Model performance is reported by Pearson $r$ and root-mean-square error (RMSE) for linearity and prediction error, by Bland-Altman bias and 95\% limits of agreement for method interchangeability,\cite{bland1986statistical,giavarina2015understanding} and by sensitivity, specificity, and accuracy at six soil-Pb regulatory thresholds: 80~ppm (DTSC residential), 200~ppm (EPA residential), 320~ppm (California CHHSL industrial), 500~ppm (DTSC industrial), 800~ppm (EPA industrial), and 1000~ppm (California TTLC).\cite{epa2024residential,dtsc2024ttlc} The proportional ash-matrix correction slope (clay-to-ash transfer) was leave-one-out cross-validated to keep extreme-leverage samples from dominating the fit; the empirical line-selection sweep, Cook's distance--based outlier handling for the matrix-correction step,\cite{cook1977detection} and validation-strategy comparisons are reported in the Supporting Information.

\section{Results}

ICP-MS analysis quantified more than 50 elements in the 39 analyzed ash samples (Tables~\ref{tab:major_elements} and~\ref{tab:trace_metals}; complete dataset in Supporting Information). Major elements were dominated by calcium, iron, and aluminum (means 52,728, 36,577, and 36,613~ppm; Table~\ref{tab:major_elements}). Hazardous metals showed pronounced spatial heterogeneity, with lead (CV~=~464\%, range 14--18,528~ppm), zinc (CV~=~292\%), and copper (CV~=~284\%) spanning two to three orders of magnitude across samples (Table~\ref{tab:trace_metals}).

Ashes from within and southwest of the urban burn perimeter showed high concentrations of Pb contamination and other harmful metals like cobalt (Co), copper (Cu), and zinc (Zn) (Figure~\ref{fig:spatial_map}). The major elements and their abundance in the Eaton Fire ashes are generally consistent with previous WUI and wildland fires.\cite{jech2024marshall,villarruel2024bioaccessible,pereira2010heavymetals,abraham2017postfire,campos2016eucalyptpine} Elements with the highest concentrations like aluminum (Al), calcium (Ca), and iron (Fe) are important ash constituents with ecosystem-scale variation due to local geology, vegetation, and soil mineral contributions.\cite{sanchezgarcia2023ashchemistry} Elements with the highest concentration in the Eaton Fire ashes like Al and Fe reflect the local geological regime of native minerals from the surrounding San Gabriel Mountains. These geogenic elements are present in relatively the same amount across all samples and provide an internal reference demonstrating that observed spatial variability in Pb and other metals is unlikely to arise from pre-fire soil heterogeneity alone (Figure~\ref{fig:icpms_summary}). Pb and other pyrogenic elements show extreme variation across all samples and are enriched in areas where materials in the built environment are combusted (Figure~\ref{fig:icpms_summary}, Figure~\ref{fig:spatial_map}).

Four calibration approaches (pellet/powder $\times$ L-line intensity / FP concentration) were compared on 37 paired ash subsamples (34 pellet, 36 powder; Table~\ref{tab:xrf_validation}). The \textit{pellet L-line intensity model} emerged as the best Pb predictor ($R^2 = 0.998$, RMSE = 155~ppm), followed by powder L-line intensity ($R^2 = 0.997$, RMSE = 271~ppm) which trades precision for field portability. Both fundamental-parameters concentration models showed high correlation ($R^2 \geq 0.97$) but $\sim$10$\times$ wider Bland-Altman agreement intervals (Figure~\ref{fig:bland_altman}) and persistent under-prediction bias, disqualifying them from quantitative use at lower regulatory thresholds. Full performance statistics, residual analyses, the line-selection sweep, and outlier-handling sensitivity are in the Supporting Information.

Both intensity models classified ash samples against the regulatory Pb thresholds with high accuracy (Table~\ref{tab:threshold_classification}): 100\% sensitivity, specificity, and accuracy at the 320 and 800~ppm levels, and $\geq$94\% accuracy with no false negatives at the 1000~ppm California TTLC. The pellet intensity model retained 91\% sensitivity at the lowest 80~ppm threshold (vs.\ 82\% for powder and 73\% for the FP models), making it the recommended primary screening tool, with powder as a field-portable alternative that maintains decision-grade performance at the 320 and 800~ppm thresholds. The number of samples exceeding each threshold and the per-arm classification matrix are reported in Table~\ref{tab:threshold_classification}.

\section{Discussion}

WUI fires produce ash with a hybrid geochemical signature that cannot be predicted from vegetation combustion alone. Wildland fires of forest or chaparral fuels generate ash dominated by alkali and alkaline-earth metals, with trace metals reflecting native soil and bedrock chemistry.\cite{pereira2010heavymetals,etiegni1991woodash,sanchezgarcia2023ashchemistry} The Eaton Fire ashes characterized here exhibit a sharp dichotomy: lithogenic elements (Al, Fe, Ti, K; CV~=~31--55\%) cluster around concentrations consistent with the granitoid bedrock of the surrounding San Gabriel Mountains, while pyrogenic elements (Pb, Zn, Cu; CV~$>$~280\%) span three orders of magnitude with concentrations strongly dependent on proximity to combusted urban infrastructure. The uniformity of geogenic elements rules out pre-fire soil heterogeneity as the source of metal variability, confirming combustion of built-environment materials as the dominant driver. Pb concentrations exceeded the California Total Threshold Limit Concentration (1000~ppm) within the burn perimeter and exceeded the EPA residential screening level (200~ppm) in downwind areas with no local structure combustion, demonstrating both source enrichment from anthropogenic combustion and aeolian transport beyond the burned footprint. This hybrid signature --- geogenic background overprinted by point-source pyrogenic emissions --- is a defining feature of WUI ash that distinguishes it from non-WUI wildfire ash and motivates monitoring frameworks tailored to mixed anthropogenic-natural fuel systems.

This study advances three capabilities relative to previous WUI ash work, which has characterized post-fire metal contamination primarily in soil after the 2021 Marshall Fire,\cite{jech2024marshall} bioaccessible metals in indoor structure ash from earlier WUI events,\cite{villarruel2024bioaccessible} and metal mobility in mineral soil from forest wildfires.\cite{pereira2010heavymetals,abraham2017postfire,fernandezmarcos2022toxics} First, we report systematic spatial sampling of \textit{outdoor ash on impervious surfaces} --- sidewalks, driveways, and street gutters --- the matrix most directly accessible to residents during the post-fire re-entry window. Second, we anchor characterization to regulatory decision thresholds rather than statistical summaries: the six classification levels (80, 200, 320, 500, 800, and 1000~ppm) span the DTSC, EPA, and CHHSL residential--industrial--hazardous-waste frameworks that govern California post-fire clearance decisions.\cite{epa2024residential,dtsc2024ttlc} Third, we provide an empirically validated XRF screening protocol calibrated against ICP-MS that achieves 100\% sensitivity and specificity at the 320 and 800~ppm thresholds for the pellet-intensity model, and $\geq$94\% accuracy at the 1000~ppm threshold for both pellet and powder preparations. Together these advances move beyond demonstrating that WUI ash is contaminated to enabling \textit{rapid hazard delineation} suitable for operational emergency response.

For emergency managers and public health officials, the spatial pattern of Pb contamination has direct implications for evacuation, re-entry, and remediation decisions. Current post-WUI guidelines vary substantially in their treatment of ash-borne metal exposure (Camp Fire 2018, Marshall Fire 2021, Eaton Fire 2025), and clearance decisions are typically gated on the multi-day to multi-week turnaround of laboratory ICP-MS --- a window during which residual outdoor ash remains mobile and accessible. Two operational changes follow from this study. (i) Sample-level decisions can be made within minutes rather than weeks: a 60-second XRF measurement on a pressed pellet (or, in the field, a loose powder cup) classifies a sample relative to the 320~ppm threshold with 100\% accuracy in the validated set, supporting site-specific re-entry decisions in place of blanket perimeter-based rules. (ii) Population-level hazard mapping becomes tractable: deployment of portable XRF across the post-fire footprint can produce same-day spatial maps of Pb exceedance, enabling triage of areas requiring expedited remediation or restricted access ahead of laboratory confirmation of borderline (200--500~ppm) samples. Combined with rapid air-quality monitoring during the active fire phase\cite{baliaka2025mmwr} and the documented toxicity of Pb at low exposure levels,\cite{tong2000leadexposure,jusko2008bloodlead,levin2008leadexposures} surface-ash XRF screening completes a tiered observational framework that addresses both the inhalation and ingestion exposure pathways for the same fire.

The pellet preparation with Pb~L$\beta$ line intensity emerged as the most accurate predictor of ICP-MS Pb across the calibration range (Table~\ref{tab:xrf_validation}), achieving 100\% sensitivity and specificity at the 320 and 800~ppm thresholds. The powder preparation traded precision for portability, retaining 100\% classification accuracy at the same thresholds with broader agreement intervals (Table~\ref{tab:threshold_classification}). Two practical limitations bound the screening utility for general use. First, sensitivity at the 80~ppm California residential RSL was 91\% (pellet) and 82\% (powder); samples in the 80--320~ppm band should be referred to ICP-MS for clearance-relevant decisions. Second, the calibration was anchored to kaolinite-clay reference standards with an empirical ash-matrix transfer correction; transfer to other geological settings or to ash matrices dominated by different vehicular or industrial fuel mixtures would benefit from local re-validation. Detailed performance statistics, including Bland-Altman agreement intervals, the empirical line-selection sweep, the matrix-correction slopes, and outlier-handling sensitivity analyses, are provided in the Supporting Information.

Given the rapid global expansion of the wildland-urban interface\cite{radeloff2018wui} and the increasing frequency of fires that combust significant built-environment fuels,\cite{nasem2022wuichemistry,schissel2025emissions} environmental monitoring approaches developed for non-WUI wildfires --- focused on biomass-derived ash chemistry --- are increasingly inadequate for the mixed anthropogenic-natural fuel systems characteristic of WUI events. The two-stage XRF calibration developed here is one element of a broader monitoring toolkit that the WUI era demands: standard reference materials representative of WUI ash composition, multi-element screening protocols beyond Pb, integration of surface and air-quality monitoring on overlapping spatial and temporal grids, and regulatory thresholds explicitly designed for mixed-source contamination. Without such tailored monitoring, post-fire exposure assessment will continue to rely on laboratory bottlenecks and fire-perimeter heuristics that systematically underestimate the spatial extent of contamination and delay public-health response. The framework reported here demonstrates that operationally deployable ash characterization --- with explicit regulatory threshold classification and minutes-not-weeks turnaround --- is achievable with existing instrumentation when calibration anchors are constructed and validated transparently.
