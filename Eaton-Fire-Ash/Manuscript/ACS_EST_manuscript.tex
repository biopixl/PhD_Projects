\title{Rapid detection of metal contamination in ash on outdoor surfaces after the 2025 Eaton Fire in a Wildland-Urban Interface}

% ============================================================================
% ABSTRACT (150-200 words)
% ============================================================================

\begin{abstract}
Wildland–Urban Interface (WUI) fires combust a heterogeneous mix of vegetation and urban materials, generating ash that can contain elevated concentrations of toxic metals, posing potential post-fire exposure risks. Despite increasing frequency and severity of WUI fires, metal contamination in coarse outdoor ash remains poorly characterized, and rapid screening tools suitable for appropriate emergency response are limited. Here, we quantified the concentration and spatial variability of metals in ash collected from outdoor impervious surfaces following the 2025 Eaton Fire in Los Angeles County, California, and evaluated X‑ray fluorescence (XRF) spectroscopy for rapid field screening of lead (Pb). Thirty‑nine ash samples were analyzed by inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry (ICP‑MS), and paired subsamples were used to develop site‑specific XRF calibration models. Lead concentrations ranged from 14 to 18,528 ppm and exhibited extreme spatial heterogeneity (coefficient of variation = 464\%), with the highest concentrations from samples collected within the urban burn perimeter. Empirical calibration using cumulative Pb L‑line intensities showed strong in-sample correlation with ICP‑MS ($R^2$ = 0.994); however, leave-one-out cross-validation revealed reduced generalization to held-out samples ($R^2_{cv}$ = 0.84; $RMSE_{cv}$ = 1,232 ppm), with model stability sensitive to high-concentration samples. Bland-Altman analysis showed 95\% limits of agreement from $-$704 to +532 ppm. Despite these quantitative limitations, binary threshold classification was robust: when evaluated against regulatory screening thresholds, the calibrated model achieved 100\% sensitivity and specificity at $\geq$400 ppm, though sensitivity declined to 86\% at 200 ppm and 70\% at 80 ppm. These results demonstrate that site‑calibrated portable XRF reliably \textit{classifies} ash relative to high-level regulatory thresholds ($\geq$400 ppm) but should not be used for quantitative Pb determination or screening at lower thresholds without ICP-MS confirmation. We recommend reporting cross-validated metrics and threshold-specific classification performance when validating field screening tools for regulatory applications.\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

% Introduction should establish context, identify knowledge gaps, and state objectives
% Typically 3-5 paragraphs, ~500-800 words

The Wildland-Urban Interface (WUI) is the transition zone between wildland vegetation and the urban build environment, characterized by a complex mosaic of land cover and land use.\cite{radeloff2018wui} Fires in the WUI are fueled by a heterogeneous pre‑fire mosaic that includes vegetation (e.g., evergreen forest and chaparral) as well as manufactured materials from housing, vehicles, and urban infrastructure. In contrast to non‑WUI wildfires, which are fueled predominantly by vegetation, WUI fires involve the partial or complete combustion of anthropogenic materials such as structures and vehicles, resulting in the release of toxic compounds, including heavy metals.\cite{young2004hazards,jech2024marshall,villarruel2024bioaccessible,pereira2010heavymetals,fernandezmarcos2022toxics,terzano2021fireeffects,abraham2017postfire,alshehri2023nanomaterials,alexakis2020suburban} Despite their increasing frequency and severity, the environmental impacts of WUI wildfires remain poorly constrained, as highlighted by a recent U.S. National Academies report.\cite{nasem2022wuichemistry} Metals present in pre‑fire WUI landscapes can volatilize at high combustion temperatures and subsequently condense onto ash particle surfaces during cooling.\cite{senior2000vaporization} The contamination potential of wildfire ash is influenced by burn severity, fuel type, ash load, post‑fire climatic conditions, surface hydrology, and ash physical properties.\cite{sanchezgarcia2023ashchemistry} Trace metal concentration in ash is highly dependent on the metal content and composition of the combusted materials.\cite{pereira2010heavymetals,abraham2017postfire,etiegni1991woodash,campos2016eucalyptpine} For example, legacy lead‑based paint in pre‑1978 structures increases both the magnitude and spatial extent of lead emissions during WUI fires.\cite{schissel2025emissions} Few studies have focused on metal concentrations in outdoor ash from WUI fires which often burn older structures.

Lead (Pb) is a toxic metal that can cause damage to almost all organs and organ systems, with adverse health effects documented even at very low exposure levels.\cite{tong2000leadexposure,jusko2008bloodlead,levin2008leadexposures} The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) Regional Screening Levels (RSLs) for residential soil set a Pb threshold of 200 ppm, while California's Total Threshold Limit Concentration (TTLC) for hazardous waste classification is 1000 ppm.\cite{epa2024residential,dtsc2024ttlc} Despite increasing WUI fire frequency, no standardized field assessment protocol exists for ash contamination on outdoor public surfaces. Laboratory analysis via ICP-MS requires days to weeks, a delay during which ash remains mobile and accessible to returning residents.

The 2025 Eaton Fire in Los Angeles County destroyed over 9,000 structures in the foothill communities of Altadena, Kinneloa Mesa, and Sierra Madre, making it the second most destructive fire in California history.\cite{calfire2025eaton} Extreme Santa Ana winds (gusts $>$100~mph) drove rapid fire spread, with most structural damage occurring within 36 hours of ignition on January 7, 2025.\cite{nws2025wind}

The smoke plume from the Eaton Fire travelled South-West and deposited coarse, residual ashes for miles over the western coastline. Similar WUI fires have been studied to understand the post-fire environmental contamination of ash and soil with toxic concentrations of metals.\cite{jech2024marshall,villarruel2024bioaccessible} During the 2025 Los Angeles Fires, air quality monitors positioned miles away from the Eaton fire recorded a 110-fold increase in the hourly lead (Pb) concentrations of particulate matter particles with a diameter less than 2.5 µm (PM2.5) compared to pre-fire days.\cite{baliaka2025mmwr} The present study addresses a gap in our knowledge of the composition and distribution of metals in the coarse ash fraction remaining on outdoor surfaces following WUI fires. 

We conducted extensive, systematic sampling of outdoor ash accumulated on impervious surfaces (e.g., sidewalk, curbs) both within the burn perimeter and downwind areas where no structures were destroyed. We hypothesized that the ash samples collected within the burn perimeter would contain higher concentrations of Pb and other metals due to proximity to combusted urban infrastructure. The friable nature of ash samples in the field necessitated rapid sampling to avoid areas impacted by cleanup activities and prior to rainfall. Using the 2025 Eaton Fire as a case study, we provide a framework for assessing potential outdoor environmental exposure to ash from WUI fires. Specifically, we address three objectives: (1) quantify the concentration and spatial variability of metals in outdoor ash following the 2025 Eaton Fire; (2) develop and evaluate site-specific X-ray fluorescence (XRF) calibration models for Pb; and (3) assess the utility of calibrated XRF measurements for rapid screening against regulatory Pb thresholds.

\section{Materials and Methods}

Sampling was initiated on January 16, 2025 (9 days post-ignition), coordinated between Caltech and NASA JPL field teams with additional contributions from trained community volunteers (sampling protocol in Supporting Information \S 4). A total of 221 ash samples were collected from publicly accessible impervious surfaces (sidewalks, gutters, driveways) within and downwind of the burn perimeter, targeting areas with visible ash accumulation prior to debris removal or rainfall. Ash (5--15~mL per site) was collected using non-metallic tools to minimize contamination (Figure~2A--B). Because sampling occurred after ash had redistributed into wind-traps, limited mixing with background dust was unavoidable; concentrations should be interpreted as minimum estimates.

Thirty-eight ash samples (collected 16--25 January 2025) were selected for their spatial distribution and temporal representativeness (pre-rainfall) to proceed with elemental analysis by inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry (ICP-MS). Of these, the 33 samples with both XRF preparations available constitute the validation set used throughout the calibration analysis below; the remaining ash samples with partial XRF coverage and four soil background controls are reported as opportunistic data in the Supporting Information. We followed EPA Method 6200 and 6020B protocols in selecting sieve sizes appropriate for the determination of elemental concentrations in soil and sediment.\cite{epa6200,epa6020b} Bulk ash samples were pulverized and homogenized to grain-size $<$250~µm (Figure~2C).

The 33 fully paired ash samples were aliquoted for Pb analysis with a benchtop energy-dispersive X-ray fluorescence (XRF) spectrometer (INAM Expert 3L, RPE Institute of Analytical Control Methods, LLC). To minimize grain-size effects on XRF spectra, subsamples were homogenized by grinding and sieving to $<$106~µm, mixed with cellulose binder, and pressed into 13-mm diameter pellets (Figure~2D). XRF spectra were acquired over 60~s. Rather than relying on the instrument's fundamental-parameters (FP) semi-quantitative output, which has known limitations for heterogeneous ash matrices,\cite{ravansari2020pxrf} we developed an empirical calibration using cumulative Pb~L$\beta_{1}$+L$\beta_{2}$ line intensity (counts~s$^{-1}$). This response avoids the As~K$\alpha$/Pb~L$\alpha$ spectral interference at $\sim$10.55~keV and was selected from an empirical sweep over single-line, summed-line, and multi-predictor combinations of six Pb~L-lines (full sweep in Supporting Information \S 1). External calibration used kaolinite-clay standards spiked at 0--1000~ppm Pb (protocol in Supporting Information \S 1). Alternative calibration approaches, including loose-powder preparation and fundamental-parameters-derived concentrations, are documented in Supporting Information \S 2 for technical comparison but are not recommended for screening use.

The pellet L$\beta_{1}$+L$\beta_{2}$ intensity model was validated against ICP-MS Pb on the 33 fully paired ash samples. Model performance is reported by Pearson $r$ and root-mean-square error (RMSE) for prediction accuracy, by Bland-Altman bias and 95\% limits of agreement (LOA) for method interchangeability,\cite{bland1986statistical,giavarina2015understanding} and by sensitivity, specificity, and accuracy at six soil-Pb regulatory thresholds: 80~ppm (DTSC residential), 200~ppm (EPA residential), 320~ppm (California CHHSL industrial), 500~ppm (DTSC industrial), 800~ppm (EPA industrial), and 1000~ppm (California TTLC).\cite{epa2024residential,dtsc2024ttlc} The two-stage calibration pipeline, comprising clay-pellet calibration followed by proportional ash-matrix correction with leave-one-out cross-validation and Cook's distance-based outlier handling,\cite{cook1977detection} is detailed in Supporting Information \S 1--2.

\section{Results}

ICP-MS analysis quantified more than 50 elements in the 38 analyzed ash samples (Table~\ref{tab:trace_metals}; major elements in Supporting Information Table~S9). Major elements (Ca, Fe, Al) showed low variability (CV~=~36--115\%), while hazardous metals showed extreme spatial heterogeneity: Pb (CV~=~459\%, range 14--18,528~ppm), Zn (CV~=~288\%), and Cu (CV~=~281\%) spanned two to three orders of magnitude across samples (element-by-element summary in Supporting Information Figure~S4).

Ashes from within and southwest of the urban burn perimeter showed the highest concentrations of Pb and other hazardous metals (Cu, Zn, Co; Figure~\ref{fig:spatial_map}).

The pressed-pellet L$\beta_{1}$+L$\beta_{2}$ intensity model showed strong correlation with ICP-MS Pb across the 33 fully paired ash samples (Table~\ref{tab:xrf_validation}; Figure~\ref{fig:xrf_validation}): $r = 0.999$, RMSE = 151~ppm, Bland-Altman bias of $-$2~ppm, and 95\% limits of agreement (LOA) from $-$302 to +299~ppm (LOA range = 601~ppm).

Threshold classification performance is summarized in Table~\ref{tab:threshold_classification}. At the 320~ppm threshold, the pellet intensity model achieved sensitivity = 1.00 [0.61--1.00] and specificity = 1.00 [0.88--1.00] with no misclassifications. At the 80~ppm threshold, sensitivity was 0.91 [0.62--0.98] with 1 false negative and 1 false positive. Across all six thresholds, the model produced 3 false negatives in 34 above-threshold tests (pooled sensitivity = 0.91 [0.77--0.97]) and 4 false positives in 164 below-threshold tests (pooled specificity = 0.98 [0.94--0.99]). Comparison with alternative calibration approaches is provided in Supporting Information \S 2.

\section{Discussion}

WUI fires produce ash with a hybrid geochemical signature distinct from vegetation-only wildfires.\cite{pereira2010heavymetals,etiegni1991woodash,sanchezgarcia2023ashchemistry} The Eaton Fire ashes exhibit uniform geogenic elements (Al, Fe, Ti; CV~=~31--56\%) consistent with the granitoid bedrock of the San Gabriel Mountains, but extreme variability in pyrogenic metals (Pb, Zn, Cu; CV~$>$~280\%). This uniformity of lithogenic elements rules out pre-fire soil heterogeneity as the source of metal variability, confirming combustion of built-environment materials as the dominant contamination driver. Pb exceeded the California TTLC (1000~ppm) within the burn perimeter and exceeded the EPA residential threshold (200~ppm) in downwind areas where no structures burned, demonstrating aeolian transport of contaminated ash beyond the fire footprint.

The pellet L$\beta_{1}$+L$\beta_{2}$ intensity model achieved a Bland-Altman 95\% LOA range of 601~ppm with negligible bias ($-$2~ppm), indicating that XRF and ICP-MS measurements are interchangeable for Pb screening purposes. Alternative calibration approaches showed 10$\times$ wider agreement intervals (Supporting Information \S 2), supporting the pellet intensity method as the preferred screening protocol.

This study advances three capabilities relative to previous WUI ash work, which has characterized post-fire metal contamination primarily in soil after the 2021 Marshall Fire,\cite{jech2024marshall} bioaccessible metals in indoor structure ash from earlier WUI events,\cite{villarruel2024bioaccessible} and metal mobility in mineral soil from forest wildfires.\cite{pereira2010heavymetals,abraham2017postfire,fernandezmarcos2022toxics} First, we report systematic spatial sampling of \textit{outdoor ash on impervious surfaces}, including sidewalks, driveways, and street gutters, which represent the matrix most directly accessible to residents during the post-fire re-entry window. Second, we anchor characterization to regulatory decision thresholds rather than statistical summaries: the six classification levels (80, 200, 320, 500, 800, and 1000~ppm) span the DTSC, EPA, and CHHSL residential--industrial--hazardous-waste frameworks that govern California post-fire clearance decisions.\cite{epa2024residential,dtsc2024ttlc} Third, we provide an empirically validated XRF screening protocol calibrated against ICP-MS that achieves 100\% sensitivity and specificity at the 320~ppm threshold for both pellet- and powder-intensity models, 100\% sensitivity with 97\% specificity at the 800 and 1000~ppm thresholds, and $\geq$97\% accuracy across the 200--1000~ppm operating range for both preparations. Together these advances move beyond demonstrating that WUI ash is contaminated to enabling \textit{rapid hazard delineation} suitable for operational emergency response.

The spatial pattern of Pb contamination has direct implications for evacuation, re-entry, and remediation decisions faced by emergency managers and public health officials. Current post-WUI guidelines vary substantially in their treatment of ash-borne metal exposure (Camp Fire 2018, Marshall Fire 2021, Eaton Fire 2025), and clearance decisions are typically gated on multi-day to multi-week laboratory turnaround times during which residual outdoor ash remains mobile and accessible. Two operational improvements follow from this work. First, sample-level decisions can be made within minutes rather than weeks: a 60-second XRF measurement on a pressed pellet classifies a sample relative to the 320~ppm threshold with 100\% accuracy in the validated set, supporting site-specific re-entry decisions in place of blanket perimeter-based rules. Second, population-level hazard mapping becomes tractable: deployment of portable XRF across the post-fire footprint can produce same-day spatial maps of Pb exceedance, enabling triage of areas requiring expedited remediation or restricted access ahead of laboratory confirmation of borderline samples. Combined with rapid air-quality monitoring during the active fire phase\cite{baliaka2025mmwr} and the documented toxicity of Pb at low exposure levels,\cite{tong2000leadexposure,jusko2008bloodlead,levin2008leadexposures} surface-ash XRF screening completes a tiered observational framework that addresses both the inhalation and ingestion exposure pathways for the same fire.

The pellet L$\beta_1$+L$\beta_2$ intensity model achieved 100\% sensitivity and specificity at the 320~ppm threshold, and retained $\geq$91\% sensitivity down to the 80~ppm residential threshold (Table~\ref{tab:threshold_classification}). Two practical limitations bound the screening utility. First, samples in the 80--320~ppm band should be referred to ICP-MS for clearance-relevant decisions given the wider confidence intervals at lower thresholds. Second, the calibration was anchored to kaolinite-clay reference standards with an empirical ash-matrix transfer correction; transfer to other geological settings or ash matrices dominated by different fuel mixtures would benefit from local re-validation. For field applications where pellet pressing is impractical, a loose-powder preparation with Pb~L$\beta_1$+L$\beta_3$ offers comparable classification accuracy at the 320~ppm threshold with broader agreement intervals (Supporting Information \S 2).

Given the rapid global expansion of the wildland-urban interface\cite{radeloff2018wui} and the increasing frequency of fires that combust significant built-environment fuels,\cite{nasem2022wuichemistry,schissel2025emissions} environmental monitoring approaches developed for non-WUI wildfires, which focus on biomass-derived ash chemistry, are increasingly inadequate for the mixed anthropogenic-natural fuel systems characteristic of WUI events. The two-stage XRF calibration developed here is one element of a broader monitoring toolkit that the WUI era demands: standard reference materials representative of WUI ash composition, multi-element screening protocols beyond Pb, integration of surface and air-quality monitoring on overlapping spatial and temporal grids, and regulatory thresholds explicitly designed for mixed-source contamination. Without such tailored monitoring, post-fire exposure assessment will continue to rely on laboratory bottlenecks and fire-perimeter heuristics that systematically underestimate the spatial extent of contamination and delay public-health response. The framework reported here demonstrates that operationally deployable ash characterization, with explicit regulatory threshold classification and rapid turnaround, is achievable with existing instrumentation when calibration anchors are constructed and validated transparently.
